## Further Recommendations and Standard Issues

### Room Sizes and Educational Use
The current room sizes (from examination rooms to imaging suites) appear adequate for hospital operations. However, it is recommended to maximise spatial capacity wherever possible to accommodate the presence of students.

**Recommendation:** Design flexible spaces that can be opened up for teaching purposes and easily closed off using sliding systems (sliding doors, operable windows, or lightweight movable partitions). These elements should be fully removable when not required, creating larger spatial volumes suitable for student gatherings and observation.

### Internal Layout and Pharmacy
The internal disposition and spatial organisation remain the primary concern. Notably, the pharmacy has not yet been properly designed.

### Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) and Zoning
TCM may be considered a special zone. However, one of the fundamental organisational principles of the hospital is the physical separation of cat and dog areas. Establishing a TCM zone that serves both species simultaneously would create an undesirable exception to this rule.

### Required Special Elevation Plans
The following dedicated plans must be developed:

- **Animal Escape Prevention Protocol** — physical barriers and containment strategies
- **Three-Route Circulation Plan** — clearly delineated paths for Clients, Veterinary Staff, and Patients, including all entry and exit points
- **Support Facilities and Equipment** — location of restrooms adjacent to waiting areas, elevators, supply rooms (cleaning and general), security/IT/server rooms, energy distribution kits, freezing/cooling zones, and an ecological waste management plan
- **Thermal Comfort, HVAC, and Ventilation** — envelope design to maintain a stable annual temperature of 22°C, prevention of overheating in sensitive areas, extraction of gases, management of liquid waste and cadavers, all while minimising maintenance costs (energy and labour)
- **Accessibility** — provisions for persons with disabilities and reduced mobility

## Summary

- The hospital footprint is generous, allowing for successful future expansion at a self-determined pace.
- The primary objective is to avoid major changes to the overall size and structural framework, while improving internal zoning and spatial sizing.
- The design should concentrate core functions on the first floor as much as possible, allowing upper floors to expand organically according to future needs.
- Certain rooms should be designed with modular principles, enabling future reshaping without interfering with the primary building structure or architectural integrity.

## Standard Compliance Issues

The following items must be addressed to meet international veterinary facility standards:

- Pharmacy room must be visible and properly dimensioned on all plans.
- Walls and floors must be solid, internally impervious, and thermally massive (high inertia) to maintain stable ambient temperatures.
- Visual transparency between hospital compartments is recommended to convey an open, professional atmosphere.
- Special protocols and plans required:
  - Full animal escape control protocol
  - Dedicated client / veterinary / patient circulation routes
- Public toilets must be located adjacent to waiting areas.
- A dedicated cadaver freezing and disposal department must be provided.
- HVAC extraction must be provided for every individual chamber and room.
- Big-animal access (e.g., horses) — clarify entry routes and treatment provisions.
- Dental room / dental surgery must be included.
- Comprehensive building systems must be defined: waste management, electricity, water supply, and plumbing.
- The hospital must be organised into three clear functional sections (zones): **Client**, **Medical**, and **Patient**.
