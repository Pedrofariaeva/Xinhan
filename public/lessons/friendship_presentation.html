📐 Grammar: Describing People

Present Simple

He / She + is / has + ...

  • She is tall and thin.
  • He has short black hair.
  • She wears glasses.
  • He is very funny and outgoing.
  • She is good at English.
7 / 20

🔄 Present Simple vs. Present Continuous

Simple (habits)

  • She always helps me.
  • We usually chat after school.
  • He often shares his snacks.

Continuous (now)

  • She is helping me now.
  • We are chatting on WeChat.
  • He is sharing his umbrella.

Use always / usually / often with Present Simple!

🎤 Speaking Model

My Best Friend

Lin Mei is my best friend. We have been classmates since Grade 5. She is kind and hard-working. She has long hair and always wears a smile. She is good at English and often helps me with my homework. We both love playing badminton after school. Last week, she shared her umbrella with me when it rained. I think a good friend is someone who truly cares about you. Lin Mei is such a friend.

Count the new words she uses!

📝 Speaking Framework

How to Describe Your Best Friend (1 minute)

  • 1. Basic info: name, age, how you met
  • 2. Appearance: height, hair, glasses, clothes
  • 3. Personality: kind, funny, helpful...
  • 4. Hobbies: good at, likes to...
  • 5. A story: what you did together
  • 6. Why you like him/her: your feeling

✍️ Writing Structure

A Good Paragraph About a Friend

  • Topic sentence: Who is this person?
  • Detail 1: Appearance (looks)
  • Detail 2: Personality & hobbies
  • Detail 3: A story or memory
  • Closing sentence: Why is this friendship important?

Target length: 60–80 words (typical test requirement)

🎭 Dialogue: Making Friends

A: Hi! I'm Lily. What's your name?
B: I'm Mei. Nice to meet you!
A: Nice to meet you too! Do you like playing basketball?
B: Yes, I love it! I play every weekend.
A: Great! We can play together after school.
B: That's wonderful! Let's be friends.

Practice with a partner! Try changing the hobby.

🎭 Dialogue: Helping a Friend

A: You look sad. What's wrong?
B: I failed my English test. I feel terrible.
A: Don't worry. I can help you with vocabulary.
B: Really? That's so kind of you!
A: We can study together every Saturday.
B: Thank you! You are a true friend.

Key phrases: Don't worry. / I can help you with... / That's so kind of you!

📖 A Story About Friendship

Sharing an Umbrella ☂️

Last Friday, it rained heavily after school. I forgot my umbrella. My friend Li Hua saw me standing at the school gate. She walked over and said, "Let's share my umbrella!" Her umbrella was small, but we walked home together. We both got a little wet, but we laughed all the way. That day, I learned: a true friend is someone who gets wet with you.

Discuss: What did Li Hua do? Why was it kind? Have you ever helped a friend?
